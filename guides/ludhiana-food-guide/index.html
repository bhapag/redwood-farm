What Is Famous to Eat in Ludhiana: A Visitor's Food Guide

By the Red Wood Farm team. Updated 17 November 2026. 5 min read.

In short

Ludhiana eats classic Punjabi food: dal makhani, tandoori chicken and paneer tikka, saag with makki di roti in winter, chole, stuffed parathas with butter, and lassi. At Red Wood Farm you can order most of the curries, tandoor dishes and parathas at listed prices, while lassi, kulcha, bhature and makki di roti are not on our menu.

Ludhiana is famous for everyday Punjabi food cooked generously: dal makhani simmered for hours, tandoori chicken and paneer tikka from the clay oven, sarson da saag with makki di roti in winter, chole, stuffed parathas topped with butter, and tall glasses of lassi. Most of it started as home cooking, and restaurants simply make it in bigger pots.

This guide explains what each dish is, so a first-time visitor knows what to order. Where Red Wood Farm cooks a version of it, you’ll see our exact menu price. Where we don’t, we say so.

Which Punjabi dishes should a first-time visitor try?

Start with the dishes that show up on almost every Punjabi table.

  • Dal makhani. Whole black lentils and a few kidney beans, cooked slowly, then finished with butter and cream. It is the dish most families order without looking at the menu.
  • Butter chicken. Tandoor-cooked chicken in a mild tomato and butter gravy. It suits children and anyone who avoids heat.
  • Tandoori chicken. Chicken marinated in yogurt and spices, then roasted on skewers in a clay oven until the edges char.
  • Sarson da saag with makki di roti. Mustard greens cooked down to a thick puree, eaten with flat cornmeal bread and a lump of white butter. It’s a winter dish.
  • Chole. Spiced chickpeas, usually eaten with kulcha or bhature, or with rice at home.
  • Stuffed parathas. Wheat flatbread filled with potato, cauliflower, radish or paneer, cooked on a griddle and served with curd and pickle.
  • Lassi. A thick yogurt drink, sweet or salted.

What does Ludhiana eat in the morning?

Parathas are the classic late-morning meal across Punjab. A plate usually means one or two stuffed parathas, a cube of butter melting on top, a bowl of curd and some pickle on the side.

Our doors open at 10:00 AM. The cheapest stuffed option is Aloo Paratha with Butter (₹50). Gobi Paratha with Butter and Mix Paratha with Butter cost ₹55 each, and the Cheese Paratha with Butter is ₹60. For the curd-and-pickle side, Plain Curd is ₹125 and Rajasthani Hari Mirch Masala Pickles are ₹65.

What tandoor food is Ludhiana known for?

The clay oven is the center of Punjabi restaurant cooking. Meat and paneer go in on long skewers, breads stick to the hot inner wall, and everything picks up a smoky edge.

On our menu, Tandoori Chicken is ₹375, Punjabi Murg Tikka is ₹415, Bhatti Da Murg is ₹395 and Pindi Seekh Kabab is ₹395. Vegetarians can order Paneer Tikka at ₹375, Hariyali Tikka at ₹325 or Mushroom Tikka at ₹395. Tandoori Roti costs ₹20 and Butter Naan ₹45. Our tandoor guide goes through the full list and what to order first.

Soya chaap also deserves a mention. Grilled or cooked in a creamy marinade, it is a common vegetarian order in north Indian cities. We list it as Champ: Malai Champ and Afghani Champ are ₹275 each, Achari Champ is ₹245 and Stuffed Champ is ₹300.

Which curries and dals do people order most?

Dal makhani leads, and our Dal Makhni is ₹275. The dal makhani page covers how it’s cooked and how much to order per person.

Butter Chicken is ₹415 for a half and ₹695 for a full. If you want something less rich, Chicken Curry is ₹395 half and ₹665 full. Kadhai Paneer and Shahi Paneer are each ₹225 half and ₹375 full.

Then there are the home-style dishes that many Ludhiana families eat on weekdays:

  • Rajmah, kidney beans in an onion and tomato gravy, at ₹230. Pair it with Steamed Rice at ₹180 or Jeera Rice at ₹230.
  • Kadhi Pakoda, a yogurt and gram flour curry with fritters, at ₹230.
  • Chana Masala, our chickpea curry, at ₹275. We don’t serve kulcha or bhature, so most guests eat it with naan or rice.

What sweets is Punjab famous for?

Punjabi meals end with milk sweets. Gulab jamun, rasmalai and kheer are year-round favorites. Jalebi with rabri is a common treat, and in winter the carrot dessert gajrela and the wheat-and-ghee ball called pinni appear in most homes.

Our dessert list has Gulab Jamun at ₹35, Rasmalai at ₹45, Raj Bhog at ₹55, Kheer at ₹65 and Kesar Kheer at ₹75. Paneer Jalebi with Rabri is ₹75. Gajrela, 100 g is ₹75 and only in season. Pinni is not on our menu. For more on cold-weather dishes, read our Punjabi winter food guide.

Questions people ask

Does Red Wood Farm serve lassi?

No. Lassi is not on our menu. Our dessert list starts at ₹35 for Gulab Jamun and includes Rasmalai at ₹45 and Kheer at ₹65.

Can I get makki di roti with saag at Red Wood Farm?

Makki di roti is not on our menu. Saag (seasonal) is listed at ₹275, and it goes well with Missi Roti at ₹30 or Tandoori Roti at ₹20. Ask us on 096467 13555 whether saag is on that day.

Which single dish is Punjab best known for?

There isn't one answer. Dal makhani, butter chicken, tandoori chicken and sarson da saag with makki di roti are the dishes people name most often when they talk about Punjabi food.

Do Red Wood Farm menu prices include GST?

Yes. Every price on our menu includes GST, so the listed price is the price of the dish.
