Kitty Party Games and Themes: Quick and Funny Ideas for Your Group

By the Red Wood Farm team. Updated 17 November 2026. 4 min read.

In short

the best kitty party games are quick, need few props and get the whole table laughing, such as one-minute challenges, baby photo guessing, film charades and housie. Pick four to six games and one simple theme. Red Wood Farm hosts daytime groups on its garden lawn on Pakhowal Road.

The kitty party games that work best are quick, need almost no props and get the whole group laughing: one-minute challenges, guessing games and team games across two halves of the table. Choose four to six, add one easy theme, and a three-hour afternoon plans itself. Every idea below works at home or at a daytime booking on our lawn.

One-minute games for a kitty party

Set a 60-second timer on a phone. Each player gets one turn, and the best score wins a small wrapped prize.

  • Cup pyramid. Build a pyramid from ten paper cups, then stack them back into a single tower, all inside a minute.
  • Name ten. The host calls a category, such as things in a handbag, Ludhiana markets or film titles with the word love. Players race to name ten before time runs out.
  • Peanut shift. Move peanuts from one bowl to another using only chopsticks. Count the peanuts at the buzzer.
  • Balloon keep-up. Keep two balloons in the air for 60 seconds using one hand. Touch the ground and you’re out.
  • Card tower. Build three levels with a deck of playing cards. A slight breeze on the lawn makes this one funnier.
  • Lipstick without a mirror. Apply lipstick in a minute with no mirror, and the group votes on the neatest result.
  • Spoon and lemon dash. Walk a lemon on a spoon to the far chair and back. Drop it and you restart from the line.

These games fill the gaps between starters arriving, so keep the props in one tote bag and hand it to whoever is hosting the round.

Funny group games for ladies

Team games take longer and get louder. Save two of them for after the main course.

  • Guess the bag. Everyone drops one item from her handbag into a basket. The host pulls them out one by one and the group guesses who each item belongs to.
  • Baby photo match. Each member sends a childhood photo to the host a day before. The host prints and numbers them, and players match faces to names on a slip of paper.
  • Film charades. Split into two teams, one on each side of the table. Act out film titles with no words. Whichever team guesses more inside two minutes per round wins.
  • Song chain. One team sings a line from any film song. The other team has ten seconds to start a new song with the last letter of that line. Miss it and the point goes across.
  • Two truths and a lie. Each person says three things about herself. The table votes on which one is made up. Long-time members usually learn something new.
  • Pass the parcel with forfeits. Wrap a small prize in many layers, with a dare slip inside each layer, such as mimicking a teacher or singing two lines.
  • Housie. The old favourite for the end of the afternoon. Bring printed tickets and use a number-calling app.

Simple kitty party themes

A theme gives the group something to plan for and makes the photos match.

  • Kite theme. A daytime party on our lawn used a backdrop of paper kites on a green curtain, with flower stands in front. Kites suit a winter afternoon.
  • One-color dress code. Everyone wears yellow, pink or white. Daytime light on the lawn makes single-color group photos easy.
  • Festival month. Plan around Lohri in January, Teej in the monsoon months or the Diwali season, and match the food and the outfits.
  • Retro film day. Each member dresses as an old film star. The song chain game fits this theme well.
  • Garden picnic. Sun hats, sunglasses and a basket of fruit as the table centerpiece.
  • Shawl and scarf afternoon. A cozy winter theme for November to February, with everyone in her favourite shawl.

Running games at a daytime lawn booking

The long wooden tables on the lawn by day suit games, because the host can see everyone and be heard. The lounge seating nearby works for chatting between rounds.

A running order that keeps energy up:

  1. Late morning: arrivals, photos at the backdrop and the first one-minute games while starters come out.
  2. Middle: mains, then two team games.
  3. End: dessert, housie and the prize handover.

Order finger food that doesn’t stop play. Masala Papad (₹75), Aloo Chana Chaat (₹125), Spring Roll (₹240) and Honey Chilli Potato (₹255) work well between rounds. For a group, a Chinese Veg Platter (₹575) or a Tandoori Veg Platter (₹525) covers several tastes on one plate. Paneer Jalebi with Rabri (₹75) is a good last plate with housie. Wrap prizes before you leave home and keep them in one bag. Hand them all out at the end so nobody leaves early with a prize still owed.

Questions people ask

How many games should a kitty party have?

Four to six is plenty for a three-hour afternoon. Mix two or three one-minute games with one team game, and finish with housie or a prize round.

What prizes work for kitty party games?

Small things wrapped before the party, such as a scented candle, a small plant or a box of sweets. Keep every prize close to the same value so nobody feels short-changed.
