## What is dal makhani?

Dal makhani is a slow-cooked lentil dish from Punjab. It is usually made with whole black lentils and a smaller share of kidney beans, simmered for a long time and finished with butter and cream. The long cooking makes it thick and dark brown, with a soft, almost silky texture. The name itself refers to the butter.

On our menu the dish is spelled Dal Makhni, so look for that name when you order. If you want to know how our kitchen cooks it, or how spicy it is on a given day, ask us on WhatsApp and we'll tell you.

## Which state is famous for dal makhani?

Punjab. Dal makhani grew up in the same North Indian kitchens as the tandoor, and it is often served at Punjabi weddings, family dinners and festival meals. Around Ludhiana it is a regular on restaurant menus, and many families also cook a version of it at home for Sundays and guests.

Because it comes from this region, people here tend to have firm opinions about it. Some like it smoky and heavy on butter. Others want it lighter with more bite left in the lentils. Try ours and tell us what you think.

For a home party where dal is one of several dishes, a common rule of thumb is one small side bowl per adult. Plan for more if dal and rice are the main meal. Plan for less when two or three other gravies share the table.

## Is dal makhani healthy?

It depends on how much you eat and what goes with it. The lentils and beans give plant protein and fiber. The butter and cream add a lot of fat and calories, which is where the rich taste comes from. Many families keep it for weekends and celebrations and eat plainer dal during the week.

If you want to keep the meal lighter at our place, a few choices help:

- Pair it with Tandoori Roti (₹20) in place of a buttered naan.
- Add a Green Salad (₹125) or Plain Curd (₹125).
- Share one Full portion across the table and add a second, plainer dal such as Yellow Dal Tadka (₹245).

This is general information. If you follow a diet for a health condition, check with your doctor.
