## What does Indo-Chinese mean?

Indo-Chinese is Chinese cooking that has been changed to suit Indian tastes. It is commonly traced to the Chinese community in Kolkata, and from there it spread to restaurants and street stalls across India. The cooking uses Chinese methods such as stir-frying and deep-frying, with soy sauce, vinegar, garlic, ginger and green chilli. Indian cooks then add more heat and a thicker, glossier sauce.

Many dishes in this style were invented in India and are rarely found in China. Manchurian is the best-known example. In Punjab, Indo-Chinese plates are a regular part of family dinners and parties, often eaten as starters before a North Indian main course.

The words in a dish name tell you how it is cooked. "Dry" means the sauce clings to the pieces, so the dish works as a starter. "Gravy" means more sauce, meant to go over noodles or rice. "Chilli" usually signals a soy and green chilli sauce with onion and capsicum. "Crispy" means the main ingredient is fried before it is tossed in sauce.

## Soups

Four of our soups belong to this style:

- **Manchow Soup, ₹155.** Usually a dark, spicy soup with chopped vegetables, often topped with crisp fried noodles.
- **Sweet Corn Soup, ₹155.** Usually a thick, mild corn soup.
- **Hot & Sour, ₹145.** Sharp with vinegar and hot with pepper, as the name says.
- **Chicken Hot & Sour, ₹245.** The non-veg version.

A soup is a good way to start a winter evening on the lawn while the rest of the order is cooked.

## Manchurian, dry or in gravy

Manchurian is usually made from deep-fried balls of minced vegetables or pieces of chicken, tossed in a garlic, soy and chilli sauce. We serve it two ways.

Order the dry version as a starter to share. The gravy version is meant to go with noodles or rice. Fish Manchurian sits in our seasonal fish section, so ask us on WhatsApp whether it is available on your date.

## Building an Indo-Chinese order for your table

For a vegetarian table of three or four, one way to order is a soup each, then a dry starter and a gravy with noodles:

1. Manchow Soup, ₹155 each
2. Honey Chilli Potato, ₹255
3. Manchurian Gravy, ₹260
4. Hakka Noodles, ₹255

For a mixed table, swap in Chicken Manchurian (₹355) and Chicken Noodles (₹345). If someone at the table doesn't eat chilli, French Fries (₹195) sit on the same appetizer list. Our [kids' favorites page](../kids-favourites/) has more ideas for younger diners.

You can also order one or two Indo-Chinese starters and then move on to Butter Chicken or Dal Makhni with naan. Both styles come from the same kitchen, so a mixed meal is easy to put together.
