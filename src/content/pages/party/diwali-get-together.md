## When do people hold Diwali get-togethers?

Most families spend the festival night itself at home, with prayers, visits to relatives and boxes of sweets going from door to door. The get-togethers happen around it. Offices hold a party in the week before. Friends meet for lunch on the weekend. The extended family sits down to dinner a day or two after, once the visiting is done.

If you can, pick a date other than the festival night. Guests are freer, and you aren't competing with every family's own plans. Our regular hours are 10 AM to 11 PM every day. For the festival days themselves, confirm timings with us on WhatsApp.

## Family dinner or office party?

### A family Diwali dinner

Families usually want an evening, and the garden's own lights do much of the decorating. There is a walk-through tunnel of warm white fairy lights, palms along the driveway wrapped in lights, more trees wrapped in lights and lamp posts along the garden paths. Arrive just after dark and the place is already glowing.

Grandparents should know before they come that Red Wood Farm has no wheelchair access.

### An office Diwali party

Offices tend to prefer lunch on a working day close to the festival, as a thank-you to the staff. An afternoon on the lawn gets the team out of the building for a few hours, and the long wooden tables set out in daytime let everyone sit together. ## What savory food suits a festive table?

Many Punjabi households keep the festival day vegetarian, while the parties around it often include chicken. For a mixed group, build a vegetarian-heavy spread and add one or two non-veg dishes.

**Vegetarian:** Paneer Tikka (₹375), Mushroom Tikka (₹395), Shahi Paneer (₹225 half, ₹375 full), Dal Makhni (₹275), Veg Biryani (₹325), Garlic Naan (₹55) and Mixed Raita (₹175).

## How do you run the evening from arrival to dessert?

A Diwali get-together runs more smoothly with a loose order to it. Here is one way to plan a family evening on the lawn.

- **Arrive around dusk.** The lights come into their own as the sky darkens, and guests get photos in their festive clothes while there is still a little light.
- **Photos first.** Walk the family through the fairy-light tunnel before anyone sits down. Once the food arrives, nobody wants to get up.
- **Starters while people trickle in.** Order a Tandoori Veg Platter (₹525) or Paneer Tikka (₹375) so early arrivals have something to share.
- **Mains once the last car is in.** Call for the dal, the curries and the breads when the whole family is seated.
- **Sweets last, and slowly.** Order the desserts as a separate round, so they arrive as the centerpiece of the evening.

Share this plan when you book, and we will tell you what works on the day.
