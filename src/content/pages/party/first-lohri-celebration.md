A baby's first Lohri is usually a family gathering in mid-January: grandparents, aunts, uncles and cousins come together to bless the baby, sing, share winter sweets and eat a warm meal. Plan it for the late afternoon or early evening, keep it short enough for the baby, and choose food that suits both elders and children.

## Why the first Lohri matters to Punjabi families

Lohri marks the end of the coldest stretch of winter and the start of the harvest season. For a family, the first Lohri after a baby is born, or after a wedding, is a reason to invite everyone. Relatives bring blessings and small gifts, and the evening is about the new member of the family. Many families now celebrate a daughter's first Lohri with the same warmth as a son's.

## When to hold it, and for how long

Lohri usually falls on 13 January, the evening before Maghi. Some families gather on the day itself. Others pick the nearest weekend, so relatives from outside Ludhiana can travel.

With a small baby, timing matters more than anything else:

- start in the late afternoon or early evening, while it is still mild
- plan for two to three hours, not a late night
- have the blessings and songs early, when the baby is fresh
- keep a quiet corner where a parent can feed or settle the baby

## Keeping the baby comfortable on a January evening

Our tables are on an open garden lawn, and Ludhiana evenings in January are cold. Dress the baby in layers with a cap and socks, and bring a blanket for the pram or the lap. Elders feel the cold too, so tell guests in the invitation to bring a shawl or a jacket. If you want a covered area, ask us what seating is available on your date. A covered tent with chandeliers has been used at past events, and whether one suits your date is a question to ask when you book.

## First Lohri ideas that need no special set-up

You don't need a theme to make the evening feel special. A few simple ideas families use:

- **A blessing circle.** Grandparents hold the baby first, then each relative gives a blessing in turn.
- **Lohri songs.** Ask an aunt or a grandmother to lead the traditional songs. Print the words for younger cousins.
- **Winter sweets to share.** Rewri, gajak, peanuts and popcorn are the classic Lohri treats. Ask us before bringing any from outside.
- **A photo walk.** The fairy-light tunnel over a red carpet makes a good backdrop for a family photo with the baby, as long as the baby is wrapped up warm.
- **A note for the baby.** Ask each guest to write one line for the baby to read when they grow up.

## How a first Lohri differs from a regular Lohri party

A general [Lohri party](../lohri-celebration-ludhiana/) can run late with friends and music. A baby's first Lohri is shorter, centred on the family, and planned around one small guest. Fewer people, an earlier start and simpler food usually make it a calmer evening for the parents.
