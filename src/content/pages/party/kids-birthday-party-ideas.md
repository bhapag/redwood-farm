The kids birthday party ideas that work in Ludhiana are simple. Keep the party to two or three hours, plan games that need running space, order food children already eat, and have enough adults watching. An open lawn handles the running games a flat can't. These ideas work at home or at Red Wood Farm on Pakhowal Road.

## Home party or venue: which suits your child?

A party at home makes sense when the list is short and mostly cousins. You know where everything is, toddlers can nap in their own beds, and nobody needs directions. The cost is your time. Someone cooks, someone cleans up, and a dozen children end up racing between the sofa and the dining table.

A venue starts to make sense once school friends join, because most of their parents stay too. The food comes out cooked, staff clear the plates, and the mess stays behind when you drive home. At a garden venue the children also get real grass under their feet.

A rough guide:

- **Ten or fewer children, mostly family:** home is easy.
- **A whole class plus parents:** look for a place with open outdoor space.
- **A winter evening party:** ask the venue where guests sit once the night turns cold.
- **Grandparents who use a wheelchair:** Red Wood Farm has no wheelchair access, so a home party or a step-free venue may suit your family better.

For the venue side of the choice, our page on [birthday party venues in Ludhiana](../birthday-party-venues-ludhiana/) covers what to check before you book.

## Games that work on an open lawn

Indoor games run out of space fast. On grass the children can spread out. Most of these need little more than a phone and a few things from home:

- **Picture-clue treasure hunt.** Draw 8 to 10 clues for children who can't read yet, and write words for older ones. Hide them near your own tables, within sight of the adults. Check with us on WhatsApp before the day about where clues can go.
- **Musical statues.** One phone and a small speaker. Everyone freezes when the music stops. It works from about age three up to ten.
- **Relay races.** Spoon and lemon, a sack race in old pillow covers, or a plain run to the far chair and back. Swap the lemon for a small soft ball with the little ones.
- **Bedsheet bounce.** Four parents hold an old bedsheet by the corners and bounce a soft ball while the children try to catch it.
- **Bubble corner.** Two bottles of bubble mix keep toddlers busy while the older kids race.
- **Card-making table.** Put crayons and paper on one table. Each guest draws a card for the birthday child, and you take home a stack of keepsakes.

For a theme, pick one color and one character, then carry them through the cake, the balloons and the return gifts. Birthday set-ups with balloon arches have been done on our lawn. Tell us what decoration you plan to bring when you book, and we'll confirm on WhatsApp what works.

## Planning a party for about 20 children

Twenty children rarely means twenty guests. Count the parents who will stay, the siblings and the grandparents. Send us both numbers, children and adults, on WhatsApp, and we'll tell you which part of the lawn suits your group on that date. There is no fixed number we quote, because it depends on the set-up.

A simple three-hour afternoon plan:

1. **First 30 minutes:** arrivals, name stickers and the card-making table.
2. **Next 45 minutes:** two or three running games while energy is high.
3. **Then food:** starters for the children, mains for the adults alongside.
4. **Cake:** after the food, once the children have calmed down a little.
5. **Last half hour:** one quiet game, return gifts and goodbyes.

We open at 10:00 AM every day, so a late-morning party works too. For quantities, use our [party planning checklist](../party-planning-checklist/).

## Safety notes for an outdoor kids' party

- Red Wood Farm has no play area, rides or kids' zone. The lawn is open grass, so plan supervised games and give each small group one adult.
- Pick up burst balloon pieces from the grass. They are a choking risk for children under three.
- Ask children to keep their shoes on during races.
- Write a parent's phone number on each name sticker.
- Ask parents about food allergies before you order, and share the menu link ahead of the day.
- On warm days, bring water bottles and caps. After dark the paths are lit by lamp posts and string lights, but keep the little ones close to your tables.
- Keep games away from the walkways where food is carried out.
