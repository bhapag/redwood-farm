A common rule of thumb for an Indian party is 6 to 8 starter pieces, 2 to 3 breads, one small bowl of dal and about 100 to 150 grams of each main dish per adult guest. Count children under ten as roughly half an adult. Treat these as general planning figures, separate from Red Wood Farm's own portion sizes.

## Food per guest: a quick reference

These quantities are general guidance used widely for home parties and catering in North India. They are starting points to adjust for your crowd. They are not the portion sizes of any dish on our menu.

| Item | Per adult guest (general guidance) | Adjust when |
|---|---|---|
| Starters | 6 to 8 pieces across all varieties | Starters run long before dinner: go higher |
| Dal | About 100 to 150 g cooked, one small bowl | Two dals on the menu: split the amount |
| Each main dish (paneer, chicken, mutton) | About 100 to 150 g | Four or more mains: reduce each |
| Rice | About 100 to 150 g cooked | Rice is the main carb: go higher |
| Breads | 2 to 3 pieces | Mostly naan: go lower |
| Raita | About half a cup | Hot weather: go higher |
| Salad | About 50 to 75 g | Heavy starters: go lower |
| Dessert | 1 to 2 pieces, or one portion | Two desserts: smaller of each |

A few patterns hold at most parties. Guests who wait a long time for dinner tend to eat more than the table suggests. Lunch parties usually eat a little less than dinner parties. People eat more of the first dish served and less of the fifth, so a long menu needs smaller quantities of each item.

## Buffet or served meal: how the quantities change

The difference is small. At a buffet, guests take a spoon of almost everything, so each dish needs to cover most of the room even if it is a side. Plan the full per-guest figure for every main. When food is served to the table, hosts can watch what runs out and order more, so it is easier to start lower and add.

Leftovers are the other side of the same sum. Ordering mains in smaller portions first and adding a second round wastes less than ordering everything large at the start. Ask your venue how quickly a second round can come out.

## How much dal makhani per person?

As general guidance, plan about 100 to 150 grams of cooked dal makhani per adult, which is roughly one small bowl. If it is the only dal, stay near the top of that range, because many guests take a second helping with naan. If there is a second dal or a kadhi on the menu, plan less of each.

## Headcount: build in a buffer

Food quantities only work if the headcount is right. A few habits help:

- **Count confirmed guests, then add a buffer.** Many hosts plan for 5 to 10 percent more than their confirmed number to cover extra arrivals.
- **Split veg and non-veg early.** It changes how many of each main you need.
- **Count children separately.** Small children eat far less than adults, and teenagers often eat more.
- **Give the venue a final number** two or three days before the party, and ask how late you can change it.

## A simple party planning timeline

**Four to six weeks before:** fix the date, a rough guest list and your budget. Shortlist venues and check availability. Our guide on [choosing a party venue in Ludhiana](../../guides/how-to-choose-a-party-venue-ludhiana/) helps with this step.

**Two to three weeks before:** book the venue, send invitations and pick a theme or decoration plan. Shortlist the menu.

**One week before:** chase replies, confirm the cake and any decorator, and settle the menu.

**Two to three days before:** give the venue your final headcount and veg split. Share the location pin with guests.

**On the day:** arrive early to check the set-up, keep one phone number for the venue, and name one family member to handle questions.

For costs, our [birthday party budget guide](../../guides/birthday-party-budget/) shows how to plan the spend line by line.

## What to ask a venue before you book

- Is the space private for your group, and does it fit your headcount comfortably?
- What happens if the weather changes, for an outdoor area?
- When does your slot start and end, and can you arrive early to set up?
- Can you see the menu in writing, with prices, and is GST included?
- Are veg and non-veg dishes clearly marked?
- What decoration, cake or music can you bring?
- How much is the advance, and what happens if you change the date?
- Is there wheelchair access for older guests?
- Who is your contact on the day?
